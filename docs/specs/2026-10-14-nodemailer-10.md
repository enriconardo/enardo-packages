# `@enardo/email-nodemailer`: upgrade nodemailer to 10

## Why

The item `chore/email-nodemailer/nodemailer-10` of `docs/backlog.md`. It started as `chore/email-nodemailer/architectural-assessment`, from the spec of `chore/repo/adapter-drivers` (2026-10-13). The user split it in two after the architectural review of `@enardo/email-nodemailer` (2026-10-14): this upgrade, and `fix/email-nodemailer/contract-compliance`.

- **Advisories.** nodemailer 6.10.1 has 15 advisories. Five are high: three denials of service in the address parser, a file read through the `raw` option, and TLS left unchecked on OAuth2 token requests. Among the others are SMTP command injection and mail to an unintended domain. Read together, their `patched_versions` all close from 10.0.6. **Amended after review**: the first version listed the injection and the unintended domain as high, which they are not.
- **Types.** The adapter's published declarations import nodemailer's types. 6.x has none of its own: they come from `@types/nodemailer`, a devDependency of the adapter, so a consumer that installs the adapter from a registry gets `any`. nodemailer 10 ships its own declarations.

This branch changes versions only. The adapter's gaps against `@enardo/email`'s contract are the next item's: the probes below find every one of them unchanged on 10.1.0.

## Probes

They ran on `master` (`9027b43`).

- **Setup.** A throwaway clone in the `en-probe` volume, in containers of the dev image (`en-monorepo-dev`: Node 24.21.0, pnpm 11.1.3, TypeScript 5.9.3). The registry consumers ran in `node:24-bookworm`.
- **Builds.** The clone's `dist/` was built with `pnpm exec turbo run build --filter=@cambusa/api-server... --force`, which covers the adapter and every package the api-server uses. The repo's own `dist/` of the adapter was rebuilt first with `pnpm exec turbo run build --filter=@enardo/email-nodemailer... --force`.
- **The change under probe.** In the clone, `nodemailer` went to `^10.1.0` in the three places that name it, and `@types/nodemailer` left the adapter, followed by `pnpm install`.

### Resolution and lockfile

- **`^10.1.0` resolves to 10.1.0.** 10.1.1 was published on 2026-10-14 at 05:38 UTC. pnpm holds it back as immature: asking for `nodemailer@10.1.1` installs it only by adding it to `minimumReleaseAgeExclude` in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` ("loose mode allowed these immature versions").
  - 10.1.1's only change is in nodemailer's `fetch`: it strips credentials on a cross-origin redirect (its `CHANGELOG.md`).
  - In 10.1.0's `dist/esm`, `fetch` is imported by the OAuth2 client (`xoauth2`), by content resolution (`shared`, `mime-node`) and by the entry point.
  - The api-server authenticates with `user` and `pass`, and the adapter passes its contents as strings. **Amended after review**: the first version said the adapter authenticates with `user` and `pass`. The adapter hands on whatever `transport` its caller gives, OAuth2 included, which reaches `fetch` through `xoauth2`.
- **The lockfile diff** touches two importers, the adapter and the api-server: `nodemailer` goes from 6.10.1 to 10.1.0, and `@types/nodemailer@6.4.24` leaves the adapter and the lockfile. No other entry is added or removed: nodemailer 10.1.0 has no dependencies.

### Breaking changes

From the `CHANGELOG.md` of 10.1.0, every *BREAKING CHANGES* section between 6.10.1 and 10.1.0:

| Version | Change | The adapter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7.0.0 | SES v2 SDK only; SES rate limiting and idling removed | uses SMTP |
| 8.0.0 | the error code `NoAuth` is renamed `ENOAUTH` | no code in the repo reads a nodemailer error code: `git grep` of `NoAuth`, `ENOAUTH`, `EAUTH`, `EENVELOPE`, `ESOCKET` and `ECONNECTION` outside `docs/` finds nothing |

### Build, types and lint

- **The adapter's `dist/` does not change.** `dist/index.mjs` and `dist/index.d.mts` built in the clone against 10.1.0 have the same SHA-256 as the repo's, built against 6.10.1 and `@types/nodemailer`. `nodemailer` stays external.
- **The checks pass.** `typecheck`, `lint` and `format:check` pass on the adapter and the api-server: `turbo run … --force`, 31 tasks successful. The adapter's imports (`Transporter` from `nodemailer`, `SMTPTransport` from `nodemailer/lib/smtp-transport`) resolve to nodemailer 10's own declarations, and the api-server's `transport` options (`host`, `port`, `secure`, `auth`) compile against them.
- **A registry consumer gets the types.** The consumer installs `@enardo/email` and the adapter from tarballs packed in the clone (`pnpm --filter … pack`), with nodemailer and no `@types/nodemailer`. It writes `transport: { hots: "smtp.example.test", port: 25 }`.
  - With nodemailer 6.10.1 (`skipLibCheck`, `nodenext`), the typo compiles.
  - With 10.1.0, it is TS2353, `'hots' does not exist in type 'SMTPTransportOptions'`, under `nodenext` and `bundler`, with `skipLibCheck` on and off.
  - With `host`, the consumer compiles under `nodenext` with `skipLibCheck: false`, so nodemailer's declarations check clean.

### Runtime

- **One copy.** From the api-server's folder, the api-server and the adapter's `dist/index.mjs` resolve `nodemailer` to the same `.pnpm/nodemailer@10.1.0`, and `import("@enardo/email-nodemailer")` loads.
- **Behavior is unchanged.** The repo's `dist/index.mjs`, run against 6.10.1 and against 10.1.0 with a stream transport and a minimal SMTP server, gives the same result on every row:
  - a display name that adds a recipient on `to`, and becomes header syntax on `replyTo`;
  - an address parsed as a list;
  - a line break in `subject` folded into a space;
  - an empty `to`: resolves on a stream transport, rejects with `EENVELOPE` over SMTP;
  - no sender: `MAIL FROM:<>`;
  - one recipient refused out of two: resolves;
  - the only recipient refused: rejects with `EENVELOPE`;
  - a frozen recipient object passed to nodemailer: `TypeError`.

  These are the gaps of `fix/email-nodemailer/contract-compliance`. The probe ran in the architectural review, and its rows are in that item.
- **Errors keep their shape.** **Added after review.** The adapter's `dist/` sent through 6.10.1 and through 10.1.0 four times:
  - with a refused connection;
  - with a host that does not resolve;
  - against a server that answers `535` to `AUTH`;
  - with `auth: { user }` and no `pass`.

  Each time it rejects with a plain `Error`, and the `message`, `code` and `command` are the same on both versions: `ESOCKET`/`CONN`, `EDNS`/`CONN` (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND …`), `EAUTH`/`AUTH PLAIN` (`responseCode` 535), `EAUTH`/`API`. The one difference is a new own field in 10.1.0, `originalCode` (`ECONNREFUSED`, `ENOTFOUND`), on the socket and DNS errors.
- **A pooled transporter.** nodemailer 10's own types accept `transport: { pool: true }`, which `@types/nodemailer` 6.4.24 refuses (TS2353). With `pool: true`, a transporter that the adapter creates keeps a socket open after a send, and the process does not exit (killed by `timeout` after 8 s; without `pool`, it exits at once). See *Decisions*.
- **Advisories.** `pnpm audit --json` on the clone reports no `nodemailer` advisory, and 23 in all (38 on `master`).

## Decisions (to confirm at checkpoint 1)

1. **`^10.1.0`, not `^10.0.6`.** 10.0.6 closes the 15 advisories of 6.10.1. **Amended after review**: the first version said it closes every advisory, and it does not. `GHSA-g57g-f23g-4646` (moderate, a quoted local part that gives a malformed envelope recipient, published on 2026-09-29) covers `>=9.1.0 <10.0.9`. That is an advisory 6.10.1 does not have, so reading the installed version's advisories missed it. With `npm audit` on a lockfile of each version, 10.0.6 and 10.0.8 report it, and 10.0.9 and 10.1.0 report none, so 10.0.9 is the lowest version with no advisory. A range of `^10.0.6` would have let in three versions that carry it. Beyond that, 10.1.0 is the version that was probed and installed, and a peer range names what the adapter was built and tested with. 10.1.1 joins through a lockfile refresh within the range once pnpm stops holding it back; no `minimumReleaseAgeExclude` entry is added for it.
2. **The pooled transporter is left to `fix/email-nodemailer/contract-compliance`**, which gives the adapter a `close()`, or refuses `pool`.
   - This branch makes `pool: true` compile. At runtime, `createTransport` accepted it before as well, through a cast.
   - No consumer sets it: the api-server passes `host`, `port`, `secure` and `auth`.
   - The alternative is `transport?: Omit<SMTPTransport.Options, "pool">` here: a change to the public API, which the next branch would replace.
3. **No new tests.** The package has none, and no test runner. The built files are byte-identical, and the behavior probe gives the same rows on both versions. The adapter's tests come with `fix/email-nodemailer/contract-compliance`, test-first.
   - The rules this branch relies on are checked by *Verification*, not by a test: one copy of the driver, types for a registry consumer, no advisory.
   - A type test of the consumer's view would need a packed tarball and a registry install, which no test of the repo does today.
